Key takeaway

What is design thinking?

Design thinking is a human-centered, creative, and iterative approach to solving problems by understanding people's needs, exploring ideas, and testing possible solutions.

This is an original instructional synthesis, not a direct quotation from any one author. The five-mode teaching framework is associated with the Stanford d.school, while the broader ideas have multiple intellectual contributors.

Attribution and further reading

Sources behind the video

These references credit the intellectual foundations and teaching model used in this introduction. They are not presented as sources for a verbatim definition.

01 · Tim Brown — Human-centered design thinking

Brown, T. (2009). Change by design: How design thinking transforms organizations and inspires innovation. HarperBusiness.

Why it matters: Explains a contemporary, human-centered approach to design and innovation.

02 · Stanford d.school — Five modes of design thinking

Doorley, S., Holcomb, S., Klebahn, P., Segovia, K., & Utley, J. (2018). Design thinking bootleg. Hasso Plattner Institute of Design at Stanford University.

Why it matters: Supports the Empathize, Define, Ideate, Prototype, and Test model. The modes are flexible, not a required linear sequence.

Read the Design Thinking Bootleg ↗

03 · Herbert A. Simon — Design as problem-solving

Simon, H. A. (1969). The sciences of the artificial. MIT Press.

Why it matters: Provides historical and theoretical foundations for understanding design as an activity concerned with changing situations toward preferred ones. Simon did not originate the modern five-mode teaching model.
